# Structure and typography of a long article

## This sample shows how headings, paragraphs, and inline emphasis work together during sustained reading.

### The rhythm of ordinary text

The first paragraph is short enough to introduce the subject. It reveals the spacing after the heading and the start of the text column.

The next paragraph is noticeably longer. As the number of lines grows, the measure, line spacing, letterforms, and density of colour become apparent. This is where you discover whether you want to keep reading or whether the page becomes tiring before the content has a chance to interest you.

The third paragraph is short again. This alternation tests whether the vertical rhythm remains steady across different text lengths.

### Emphasis within a sentence

An ordinary sentence may contain a **bold anchor**, *a change of tone*, ==a highlighted passage==, ~~an earlier wording~~, and `a short technical term`. None of these signals should disrupt the line.

The next sentence combines them: ***bold italics***, **bold text with *nested italics***, [a link with an **emphasised word**](https://obsidian.md), and ==highlighting with `inline code` nearby==. This reveals background heights, baselines, and spacing between accents.

Too much emphasis makes text noisy. This ordinary paragraph after the dense example tests the return to quiet reading.

### Section hierarchy

The third level is a regular internal section of the article. It should stand out from paragraphs without competing with the page title and introduction.

#### A clarifying subsection

The fourth level helps when a long section needs another step in its hierarchy.

##### A local subject

The fifth level is uncommon but should remain related to the rest of the scale.

###### A small structural note

The sixth level is the final step. It should not turn into an indistinguishable caption.

### Breaks and separation

This line ends with a hard break.  
The next line remains part of the same paragraph and shows a compact vertical movement.

A blank line separates the new paragraph. Here you can compare normal paragraph spacing with the preceding hard break.

A horizontal rule creates a stronger pause. Ordinary text follows so that the rule does not look like the end of the page.

### Literal characters

Sometimes Markdown symbols need to appear literally: \*asterisks\*, \_underscores\_, a \#hash, a \~tilde, and the number 1\. without starting a list.

The final long paragraph returns attention to the main material. If the typography forms a coherent system, all the preceding states feel like variations of one voice rather than a collection of unrelated components.

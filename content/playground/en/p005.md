# Lists and tasks in a long article

## This sample tests lists, numbering, and tasks within continuous text: short, long, mixed, and nested variations.

### A short list

A paragraph introduces several equally important features that are easier to see separately:

- line width;
- body text size;
- spacing between paragraphs.

The article continues after the list, showing the normal lower spacing.

### Items of different lengths

Short and long items should start on the same vertical line:

- a brief observation;
- a longer observation that spans several lines and lets you check the hanging indent, line spacing, and position of the next marker;
- a final short item.

The next paragraph reveals the boundary of the list without another heading.

### A sequence of actions

When order matters, the same material becomes a numbered sequence:

1. open the sample in Reading view;
2. look at the whole page;
3. record your first reaction;
4. compare the next version with the previous one.

The numbers should form a single column without crowding the text.

### Tasks during reading

A task list shows several states at once:

- [x] choose a document;
- [ ] check the body text;
- [ ] examine secondary elements;
- [-] postpone a disputed decision;
- [?] return to the question later.

Completed, open, and nonstandard states should remain distinguishable without losing readability.

### Nested structure

Sometimes an item expands in several ways:

1. First assess the foundation.
   - text size;
   - line length;
   - overall rhythm.
2. Then check the details.
   - [x] links;
   - [ ] tables;
   - [ ] diagrams.
3. Finally, return to the entire article.

Different markers, indentation levels, and vertical connections between lines appear together here.

### A task with an explanation

- [ ] Check a long task item whose explanation spans several lines and remains readable beside the checkbox. The second line should align with the text rather than begin underneath the control itself.

The final paragraph returns the page to prose. Lists should make the material quicker to scan without making the whole article resemble a task manager.

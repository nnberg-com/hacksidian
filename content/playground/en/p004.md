# Quotes in a long article

## This sample shows several ways to introduce another voice while preserving the overall rhythm and distinguishing the levels.

### A short quote

A paragraph leads into a brief idea that does not require its own section.

> A good page helps you keep reading.

The next paragraph immediately restores the author's voice. The spacing above and below the quote matters most here.

### A quote with several paragraphs

A long statement should remain comfortable to read even when it occupies a significant part of the screen.

> Visual style is not expressed through a single colour or typeface.
>
> It becomes visible through a sequence of decisions: line length, pauses, heading hierarchy, and the behaviour of secondary elements.

After a multi-paragraph quote, ordinary text should not seem unexpectedly narrow or too faint.

### A formatted quote

A quoted passage can contain the same inline elements as the article.

> **The main idea** may be emphasised, *the tone* changed, and the term `Reading view` set in monospace. A [link](https://obsidian.md) may appear here too.

The surrounding paragraph lets you compare the same elements inside and outside the quote.

### A quote containing a list

Sometimes the quoted material has a structure of its own:

> Observation has several layers:
>
> - a first impression;
> - a reaction to details;
> - a desire to keep reading.

The list remains part of the quote, so its markers and indentation should accommodate another level of nesting.

### A nested quote

Nesting shows a conversation between voices or a quotation within a source.

> The first voice states the initial idea.
>
> > The second voice responds within the same passage.
>
> The first voice returns and concludes the argument.

After this complex structure, the article resumes with an ordinary long paragraph. When borders, indentation, and text sizes work together, the transition remains clear without excessive decoration.

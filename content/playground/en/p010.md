# Formulas and footnotes in a long article

## This sample tests two kinds of secondary reading: mathematical expressions within an argument and notes that briefly draw the eye away from the main text.

### Inline formulas

The formula $L = 0.2126R + 0.7152G + 0.0722B$ sits inside an ordinary sentence. Its symbols should match the scale of the line without increasing the line spacing.

Several expressions together — $R$, $G$, $B$, and $L$ — test short fragments, while $0 \leq L \leq 1$ adds operators and numbers.

Ordinary text follows the dense line so that mathematical letterforms do not change the overall voice of the article.

### A display formula

When an expression becomes central to the argument, it occupies its own block:

$$
C = \frac{L_{\text{light}} + 0.05}{L_{\text{dark}} + 0.05}
$$

The paragraph after the formula tests vertical spacing and the return to the width of ordinary text.

### A formula with several lines

A system of expressions creates a taller and denser block:

$$
\begin{aligned}
L_1 &= 0.2126R_1 + 0.7152G_1 + 0.0722B_1, \\
L_2 &= 0.2126R_2 + 0.7152G_2 + 0.0722B_2, \\
C &= \frac{\max(L_1,L_2)+0.05}{\min(L_1,L_2)+0.05}.
\end{aligned}
$$

### Ordinary footnotes

A short footnote[^short] leads to one sentence, while a named footnote[^contrast] keeps a meaningful identifier in the source.

[^short]: A short explanation at the bottom of the article.

[^contrast]: Contrast is the ratio of luminance between light and dark areas, not simply the difference between two colour codes.

The text between the reference and the footnote area should remain the primary reading route.

### A multiline footnote

A longer note[^long] tests wrapping, the hanging number, and several lines of secondary text.

[^long]: The first line introduces the main idea.
    The second line continues the explanation with an indent.
    The third reveals a true multiline block.

### An inline footnote

Sometimes a short remark is easiest to keep directly in the source line.^[An inline footnote appears with the other notes in Reading view.]

The final long paragraph brings the reader back to the article. Footnotes should remain accessible without creating a competing text stream until the user chooses to visit them.
